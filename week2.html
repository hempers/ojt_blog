Week 2 – PC Setup & Printer Maintenance

February 23 – February 28, 2026

ALINDECO IT Department 44 Hours Logged Hardware & Systems
Weekly Narrative

What Happened This Week

The second week of my internship began with a hands-on deployment task. On Day 4, we were assigned to set up a company computer using a backup system unit. The original unit of the employee had issues, so a replacement unit was prepared to ensure continuous workflow. I assisted in preparing the backup PC by checking its hardware condition and ensuring that it was properly functioning before deployment. After confirming that the system unit was operational, we installed the necessary printer drivers to allow the computer to communicate with the office printers. We made sure that the drivers matched the specific printer models used in the department and tested print functionality to confirm proper installation. We also restored important files from the previous computer. This involved transferring essential documents and user data to the backup unit while ensuring that no critical files were lost. Proper file organization was observed to maintain the employee's workflow continuity.

On Day 5, we continued working on the same unit by adding SSD storage to the computer to improve its overall performance. We then updated the Windows version and installed Microsoft Office. Both Windows and MS Office were properly activated to ensure full functionality. Upon completing all the necessary configurations, we joined the computer to the company domain and renamed it to correspond to the name of the actual user of the computer, following the company's naming convention for network devices.

Day 6 started with the deployment of the system unit that was configured the previous day, now equipped with the updated Windows installation and the additional SSD storage. After the deployment, we began conducting a PC inventory across each department. We visited the current excel file our mentor have as a record of the units in the company. I proposed to use a QR code system to improve the inventory process. By generating unique QR codes for each computer and attaching them to the respective units, we can quickly scan and view the units information. As of now, the inventory is not yet done as all employees here are busy using their respective computers.

The second half of the week shifted toward printer maintenance. On Day 7, we replaced the toner cartridge of a Brother DCP-L2540DW series printer and cleaned the ink waste pad of an Epson L120 printer assigned to the Human Resource Department, Ms. Cristy Lim. These tasks introduced me to the routine upkeep that IT staff perform to keep office printing equipment running smoothly.

On Day 8, we continued with printer maintenance by cleaning the ink waste pad of another Epson L120 unit. We then moved on to a more involved task — disassembling an HP Deskjet Ink Advantage 3635. The printer was experiencing ink pooling caused by waste ink pad saturation, so we carefully took apart the case and individual parts to clean them thoroughly. After finishing the cleaning, we reassembled the printer and ensured it was functioning properly.

The last day of the week, Day 9, was dedicated to troubleshooting the same HP Deskjet Ink Advantage 3635 which was now prompting an E4 error. Upon replacement of the encoder strip, the error changed from E4 to E3. We tried several resolutions but were still unable to fully resolve the issue. The likely cause of the problem is a faulty encoder sensor — the encoder strip works in tandem with the sensor to track the precise position of the print carriage. If the sensor is damaged, dirty, or misaligned, it cannot properly read the encoder strip, which results in the printer being unable to determine the carriage position and throwing error codes like E3 and E4. This was a valuable learning experience in understanding how closely related printer components interact and how one faulty part can cascade into multiple error symptoms.

Reflection & Insights

What I Took Away

What I Learned

This week gave me a well-rounded experience covering both PC deployment and printer maintenance. I learned the full process of setting up a backup system unit — from hardware inspection and file restoration to SSD installation, OS updating, software activation, and domain joining. On the printer side, I gained hands-on knowledge in toner cartridge replacement, waste ink pad cleaning, and full printer disassembly for deep cleaning. The troubleshooting session with the HP Deskjet 3635 taught me how encoder strips and encoder sensors work together to control print carriage positioning.

Challenges Encountered

The biggest challenge this week was troubleshooting the HP Deskjet Ink Advantage 3635. After replacing the encoder strip, the error code shifted from E4 to E3 but the issue persisted. Despite trying multiple resolutions, we were unable to fully fix the printer. This experience showed me that hardware troubleshooting doesn't always end with a clear solution — sometimes identifying the probable root cause (in this case, a faulty encoder sensor) is just as important as the fix itself. It reinforced the need for patience and systematic elimination when diagnosing hardware issues.

How This Week Built My Skills

By the end of Week 2, I became more confident in handling both computer deployment and printer maintenance tasks independently. The PC inventory activity improved my documentation and organizational skills, while the printer repair work sharpened my ability to diagnose mechanical issues and carefully disassemble and reassemble hardware. I also developed a better understanding of how domain joining and computer naming conventions work in a corporate network environment.
