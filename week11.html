The Experience

Week eleven focused on disaster recovery and data recovery procedures—arguably the most critical aspect of IT operations. While preventive measures are important, being prepared for worst-case scenarios and knowing how to recover is what separates prepared IT professionals from those unprepared for crisis.

Backup and recovery procedures testing.

Early in the week, we reviewed and tested existing backup procedures. I learned that having backups is useless if you can't successfully restore from them. Regular restoration testing is critical to ensure backups are actually viable. We performed several test restores to verify backup integrity.

Mid-week, we worked on disaster recovery planning—developing procedures for how IT would respond to major incidents like server failures, ransomware attacks, or natural disasters. We created runbooks for rapid recovery and established recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO).

By week's end, we performed actual data recovery exercises, simulating scenarios where users accidentally deleted important files or databases became corrupted. This week reinforced that being "disaster-ready" is not pessimism—it's professional responsibility.

Reflections & Insights

Backup Testing

A tested and verified backup is worth something. An untested backup is worth nothing. Regular restoration tests are absolutely essential to ensure disaster recovery plans will actually work.

Preparedness Critical

Disasters happen when organizations least expect them. Having tested procedures and documented runbooks separates controlled disasters from organizational catastrophes.
